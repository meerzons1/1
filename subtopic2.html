DNA and Gene Cloning in Biotechnology

Sasha Meerzon | CSIT 100

This subtopic explores how scientists copy DNA sequences in the laboratory and use gene cloning methods in biotechnology, medicine, and biological research.

Source 1

Source title and URLMolecular Cloning – Addgene
Author / organizationAddgene
AbstractAddgene's molecular cloning reference explains how researchers assemble DNA fragments and place them into vectors so that specific genetic sequences can be copied, studied, or expressed in cells. The resource introduces common cloning strategies and describes the role of plasmids, restriction enzymes, DNA ligase, and other molecular biology tools. It shows that gene cloning is different from reproductive cloning: rather than producing a whole organism, scientists work with selected DNA sequences. The page is useful for understanding how molecular cloning supports experiments in genetics, biotechnology, and biomedical research. Because Addgene is a nonprofit repository that distributes research plasmids and educational resources, its explanation connects the underlying concepts to techniques used in laboratories.

Source 2

Source title and URLPCR Cloning – Addgene
Author / organizationAddgene
AbstractThis Addgene protocol describes PCR cloning, a method that uses the polymerase chain reaction to amplify a DNA fragment before inserting it into a cloning vector. It outlines the basic workflow and explains how primers are designed to define the DNA region being copied. The resource connects two important laboratory methods: PCR produces many copies of a target sequence, while cloning places that sequence into a plasmid or other vector for later use. Researchers can then propagate, analyze, or express the inserted DNA. This source is relevant because it demonstrates how DNA cloning works as a practical biotechnology procedure rather than only as an abstract concept. It also provides a clear example of how modern molecular biology techniques build on one another.
