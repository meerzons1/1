Cloning in Computer Science: Code Clones and Digital Replicas

Sasha Meerzon | CSIT 100

This subtopic considers how the idea of cloning applies to software: duplicated code in programs and digital twins that replicate the behavior of physical systems.

Source 1

Source title and URLScalable Detection of Semantic Clones
AuthorsMichael Gabel, Lingxiao Jiang, and Zhendong Su
AbstractThis research paper investigates semantic clones in software, which are separate pieces of code that perform the same or similar functions even when their written structure differs. Unlike simple copy-and-paste duplication, semantic clones can be difficult to identify because programmers may implement the same behavior using different syntax or algorithms. The authors examine methods for detecting these similarities at scale, an important challenge for software maintenance and analysis. Identifying duplicated functionality can help developers reduce redundant code, find inconsistencies, and improve program quality. This source supports the computer science portion of the project by showing that cloning can describe repeated behavior and logic in software, not only the copying of biological material or DNA sequences.

Source 2

Source title and URLDigital Twin – National Institute of Standards and Technology
Author / organizationNational Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
AbstractThe National Institute of Standards and Technology defines a digital twin as a digital representation of a real-world entity, system, or process. Digital twins can use data from physical objects and environments to represent their conditions, behavior, or performance over time. This concept is used in areas such as manufacturing, engineering, infrastructure, and scientific research. Although a digital twin is not a literal biological clone, it shares the broader idea of creating a digital counterpart that can be studied and monitored. This source is useful because it provides an authoritative definition and helps distinguish digital replicas from duplicated software code. It also illustrates how the concept of cloning has expanded into modern computing and technology.
