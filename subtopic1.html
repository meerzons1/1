Animal Cloning and Dolly the Sheep

Sasha Meerzon | CSIT 100

This subtopic examines the development of animal cloning, the scientific process that produced Dolly the sheep, and the broader significance of somatic cell nuclear transfer.

Source 1

Source title and URLThe Life of Dolly – The Roslin Institute
Author / organizationThe Roslin Institute, University of Edinburgh
AbstractThis source from the Roslin Institute describes the life of Dolly the sheep, the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell. It explains her birth in 1996, her life at the Roslin Institute, and why her creation became a major scientific milestone. Dolly was produced using somatic cell nuclear transfer, a technique in which the nucleus from an adult body cell is placed into an egg cell whose own nucleus has been removed. The source provides historical context for understanding how cloning moved from a theoretical possibility to a demonstrated biological procedure. It is useful because it comes from the research institution directly involved in Dolly's creation and presents the event in its scientific and historical context.

Source 2

Source title and URLCloning Fact Sheet – National Human Genome Research Institute
Author / organizationNational Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI)
AbstractThe National Human Genome Research Institute explains the major forms of cloning and distinguishes reproductive cloning from therapeutic and gene cloning. The fact sheet introduces the scientific principles behind cloning and describes how researchers can create genetically similar copies of genes, cells, or organisms. It places Dolly's creation within the wider history of cloning research and explains why cloning is more complicated than simply making an identical copy. The source also discusses scientific limitations and ethical questions that accompany cloning technologies. This fact sheet is valuable for the project because it provides an accessible, research-based overview from a major genomics institution. It helps clarify terminology and gives readers a foundation for comparing animal cloning with molecular and technological forms of cloning.
